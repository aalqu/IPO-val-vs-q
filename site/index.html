Check it yourself

  1. Get the code and data: https://github.com/aalqu/IPO-val-vs-q
  2. Run the tests and rebuild every number on this page: 
    pip install -e ".[test]"
make test        # unit, data-integrity and regression tests
make results     # writes results/results.json and deal_returns.csv
make robustness  # the table above
ipo-study run --universe asia          # India, Hong Kong, China A
ipo-study valuation --universe asia    # Hong Kong comps/DCF pilot
  3. Rebuild prices from a second source and compare: make crosscheck (downloads from Yahoo Finance).
  4. Change an assumption: ipo-study run --min-mcap 10 --region US --exclude-cohort 2020-21 --bootstrap 2000
  5. Found an error? Open an issue with the deal, the field, the correct value and a source link. Corrections are logged in data/corrections.csv.

Method and limits

  • Universe: 96 listings worth $5bn or more at the offer price, 2010–2025: 81 US (including 5 direct listings) and 15 global. Excluded: Glencore (offering documents could not be retrieved) and three deals with no monthly prices at Yahoo Finance (First Data, Borouge, ADNOC Gas).
  • Every number is sourced: each deal's offer price, share count, listing date and exchange rate comes from a named document — the final prospectus, the exchange's allotment or listing announcement, or the Federal Reserve H.10 rate — with the exact words quoted (data/deal_facts.csv, checked by the test suite). Market value at the offer = offer price × all shares outstanding after the offering, before any over-allotment. Nothing is estimated: a value that cannot be sourced is left blank, and a deal whose size cannot be computed is excluded.
  • Returns: buy-and-hold total return from month-end closes (adjusted for splits and dividends), minus the benchmark's total return over the same months.
  • Traditional valuation (pilot): each IPO's EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales, P/E and dividend yield at the offer price (P/B for financials) against Damodaran's industry multiples for the IPO year and region, and a 10-year DCF with the same rules for every deal. Inputs come from the final prospectus (424B4) and SEC XBRL data; every row links to its filing.
  • Simple screens: profitability at IPO and price-to-sales against the sample median, for the deals whose last full-year revenue and operating profit are reported in SEC XBRL filings (mostly US filers). Non-US deals are left out of these screens rather than given approximate figures.
  • Quant rules: reconstructed from published research (long-run underperformance, hot-issue fade, lock-up expiry, post-lock-up momentum). They are not any fund's actual strategy.
  • Limits of the valuation pilot: 19 deals (Porsche excluded for lack of sourceable accounts), so each rule trades only a handful; trailing multiples, not the forward multiples bankers emphasise; basic post-IPO share counts (excluding options and RSUs), which flatter Facebook, Snap, Uber and Airbnb.
  • Prices: monthly closes from Yahoo Finance; nine delisted US names (no longer served by Yahoo) from a digrin.com snapshot. A cross-check against a second price source has not yet been run.
  • Asia extension: 119 listings worth $5bn+ in India (27), Hong Kong (59, including mainland companies' H-shares; A+H dual listings tagged) and mainland China A-shares (33), each against its local index, with offer prices, share counts and listing dates from HKEXnews, CNINFO and the final Indian prospectuses. Excluded: three deals with no monthly prices at Yahoo Finance (Haitong Securities H, ESR, Sinovel), ICICI Prudential Life (prospectus not retrievable) and ZJLD (US$4.5bn at the offer, under the threshold). Korean listings were removed from the study (22 Oct 2026). Revenues and profitability are not yet extracted for these deals, so the price-to-sales and profitability screens are not run for Asia.
  • Asian valuation pilot (15 deals): Hong Kong (Xiaomi, China Tower, Haidilao, Smoore, Nongfu Spring; HKEX prospectuses and allotment announcements), China A (CNNP, Foxconn Industrial Internet, CATL, Mindray, Beijing–Shanghai High-Speed Railway; CNINFO prospectuses, Mindray from its annual report because the prospectus is a scanned image) and India (Coal India, HAL, Zomato, Nykaa, Paytm; final prospectuses). Every input is printed in the source or derived from printed numbers (data/fundamentals_derivations.csv); figures are as reported, with no analyst adjustments, and a dividend is used only when the prospectus states an amount. Industry benchmarks from Damodaran's China and India files, the Emerging-markets file where a country has fewer than 10 firms in the industry, and the US January 2010 file for Coal India (no earlier India file exists).
  • Not investment advice. In-sample results, small groups, no costs.
